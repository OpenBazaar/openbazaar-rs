Two keys hold the payment

Funding

The funding lock

Escrow funding is a 2-of-2 MuSig2 Taproot key-path spend. crypto::aggregate combines only the buyer and vendor points, sorts them, and applies the unspendable BIP 341 tweak. No moderator key is aggregated. There is no script tree. Every outcome is an ordinary 64-byte key-path signature.

The two points are per-order keys, from m/1984'/coin'/1'/index'. They are not the store key. The store key, which signs the store and the listing and does not spend, is m/1984'/coin'/0'/0'. On this derivation, coin is 0 on mainnet and 1 on testnet4.

The moderator record holds a ruling key and an encryption key, not a spend key. The same record also fixes the payout script, the fee, and the policy, and both parties copy that record into the terms before funding. The two moderator keys have to differ from each other and from the buyer and the vendor.

  1. Funded Release can be signed any time the buyer is satisfied
  2. Dispute deadline Last height a letter is accepted
  3. Ruling window ends No presigned spend pays the vendor here
  4. Refund height Silence pays the buyer, minable at the next block

Mainnet stays refused Moderated escrow is not available on mainnet. It is a testnet4 demo until the two-party escrow protocol is reviewed. The node calls the demo unsafe and says not to send anything of value.

Escrow terms, version 4

What the lock can pay

The terms are signed before funding, and nothing outside them can appear in a presigned transaction. Version 4 changed the silent case: a silent moderator does not pay the vendor. The presigned claim is the buyer refund. A version 3 encoding, which paid the vendor on that claim, does not decode.

The moderator fee is paid only on a published ruling. A ruling for the vendor still pays the vendor and the fee. Cooperative release and the buyer refund do not pay the moderator. The default policy asks for at least 144 blocks from the dispute deadline to the end of the ruling window, and another 144 to the refund height.

The claim and the buyer refund are the same transaction. Release has no 240-sat anchor. The refund, the claim, and both rulings set one aside so the recipient can raise the fee later.
Outcome When it can pay Who receives the coins Moderator fee
Cooperative release After the buyer is satisfied. Locktime is zero. Signed after funding, not before. The vendor, for the funding amount minus the network fee. Not paid
Buyer refund At the refund height, the backstop if the vendor has vanished. The buyer, after the network fee and a 240-sat anchor. Not paid
Presigned claim The same refund. This is what silence pays. The buyer. It does not pay the vendor. Not paid
Ruling for the buyer Once that ruling is published. Locktime is zero. The buyer, and the moderator’s payout script. Paid
Ruling for the vendor Once that ruling is published. Locktime is zero. The vendor, and the moderator’s payout script. Paid

Settlement

One share, then the favored half

A dispute letter carries one share. That share is the moderator half of one branch, created by the party who loses that branch and encrypted to the moderator’s encryption key. The favored party’s half is derived from their per-order key and the branch sighash. It is never sent.

Settlement is those two halves added together, completing the adaptor signature both parties signed before funding. Only the favored party can finish that branch. The moderator’s share alone does not produce a valid signature, so a published secret does not pay the winner, and no one else can broadcast it.

The moderator does not sign the Bitcoin transaction. They open one letter, after checking it against their own view of the funding output, and return the decrypted share inside a ruling signed by the ruling key. A second letter for the other branch of the same escrow is refused. Two valid rulings on the same terms and outpoint are proof the moderator equivocated.

  1. 1Both parties sign the terms. Every payout, height, fee, and the moderator record are fixed there.
  2. 2They exchange one encrypted share per branch and presign the refund, the claim, and both dispute outcomes.
  3. 3The buyer funds the 2-of-2 output. The release signature comes later, if the buyer is satisfied.
  4. 4A claimant sends one letter. The moderator returns one share, for the branch the letter asked about.
  5. 5The favored party adds their own half and finishes that branch. The other party cannot.

Reputation

A count, a bond, and a ruling

The public ruling is a second record, tagged ob/public-ruling/v1, signed by the moderator’s existing ruling key. It is not the ruling the in-flight claim verifies. It cites who was paid, the store key at m/1984'/coin'/0'/0', and the listing id covered by the store-signed offer. Name, street, email, phone, coordinates, and ship-to are refused. Buyer keys are not published.

Catalog text has the same restraint. A store or listing record refuses an email, a phone number, a street, coordinates, and a postal code. People are not named in public text.

The count

0 Direct pay is refused. The sale has to be escrow, with the moderator named.
1+ Direct pay is allowed. Escrow can start without a bond.

Only a finished escrow payout counts: a cooperative release or a dispute settlement. Direct pay does not. A seller paying their own sale does not. The count is a number. Orders are not published with it.

The bond

Tag ob/bond/v1, testnet4 only. Mainnet is refused with the same sentence as mainnet escrow. The minimum is 10,000 sats. The minimum lock is 2,016 blocks, by CLTV or by CSV.

The script pays the store key after that lock, and nothing else. There is no slash key. A buyer checks the signature, the script, the minimums, and the outpoint. A new seller with no passing bond is refused when escrow starts.

Where a sale may go

Loopback, or a v3 onion

A sale URL is loopback HTTP, or http:// plus a v3 onion reached through this node’s Tor client. A clearnet host is refused before a connection. A redirect is not followed. This is not a directory and not a DHT.

The one store a downloaded test build can open without typing an address is the vendor onion below. A catalog read is GET /public/store on that kind of URL. The code also allows public media, reviews, order mail, and escrow mail on the sale onion, and it leaves wallet and spend routes closed. The clearnet listener stays on loopback.

A live GET /public/store on this onion has not been shown. This page does not claim a buyer can already browse that vendor.

What a signed listing can already say

A sale price, or an offer

A listing can carry a scheduled sale: a percentage off between two days, inclusive, signed onto the listing. It can also take a Best Offer. An accepted offer sets the unit price for that buyer and that quantity, and it does not stack with a coupon. An auction is not part of this node.

The behavior on this page is the node at 339462b. The funding aggregate is crypto::aggregate. The bond and the public ruling are ob/bond/v1 and ob/public-ruling/v1. The test-network onion is the seed in the node.
