\subsection{Adversarial review of the two-party escrow}
\label{sec:review}

The PR~\#3 escrow was reviewed adversarially by a reviewer who did not write it. The review found the cryptographic core sound: the bitwise ElGamal verifiable encryption with per-bit OR-proofs and a DLEQ link is the standard bit-decomposition approach~\citep{stadler1996pvss}; no rogue-key or cancellation attack was found; no party could obtain a signature outside the presigned set; nonces were fresh and single-use; funding was withheld until the refund and the buyer-wins pre-signature verified; and 20{,}000 fuzzed decoder inputs produced no panics. The findings were in the policy checks around that core. Table~\ref{tab:review} lists them with their status on \code{escrow-two-party} at \reviewcommit. ``Open'' means no fix was on the branch at that commit; ``fixed'' means a fix and a test for it were on the branch; ``proposal only'' means the fix is a design that is not implemented.

Our own release-build measurement of one share verification is 52~ms (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}); we did not reconcile it with the review's figure, which may count a full letter check or a different build. H1, H2, M1, M2, and M5 were classed as must-fix. We treat all open findings as blocking for any mainnet use of the two-party escrow, and adopt the review's recommendation that the protocol receive an external cryptographic review before then. The review also noted smaller alternatives to the 50~KB share, such as segmented ElGamal with range proofs as in JugglingSwap~\citep{shlomovits2020juggling}, and that Camenisch--Shoup verifiable encryption~\citep{camenisch2003} fits poorly because it needs a Paillier-style group.
